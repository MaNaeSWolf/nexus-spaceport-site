Nexus has reserved 2,000 hectares of coastline at Morrumbene, in Mozambique's Inhambane Province, as the Phase 1 site for its first spaceport.

The location sits at 23°35′48″S, 35°25′15″E — near-equatorial, ocean-facing and sparsely populated, with open Indian Ocean downrange and a coastline that turns away from the flight path rather than into it. The site is recognised within the national port development programme.

Why this coastline

Six conditions have to hold at once for a launch site to work: coastal, near-equatorial, sparsely populated, meteorologically stable, large enough for multiple pads with full safety buffers, and in a jurisdiction where commercial launch licensing is genuinely achievable. Very few places on Earth still satisfy all six, and that inventory is shrinking as coastline is consumed by settlement, tourism and industry.

Mozambique also sits on some of the largest proven natural gas reserves in Africa — the feedstock for methane, the propellant the next generation of reusable vehicles is being designed around.

What is planned

The Phase 1 configuration covers thirteen launch pads, vertical and horizontal integration facilities, return-to-launch-site landing areas, downrange recovery, on-site propellant production, a control centre, test and validation facilities, and a tenant estate on the perimeter.

Safety determined the footprint rather than the other way around: exclusion envelopes, acoustic zones and land requirements were sized before any hectare was acquired, which is why the site is as large as it is.
