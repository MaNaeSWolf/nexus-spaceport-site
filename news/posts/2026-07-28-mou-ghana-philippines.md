Nexus now has memoranda of understanding covering both of its follow-on sites,
with site selection under way at each.

**Palawan, Philippines** is the Phase 2 site — the highest-scoring location in
the Nexus global site analysis. Its near-equatorial latitude gives the strongest
delta-v advantage in the network for GEO, MEO and equatorial constellation work,
with an east-south-east corridor from the Sulu Sea into the Pacific.

**Ahanta West, Ghana** is the Phase 3 site, at Cape Three Points on the Western
Region coast. It was identified by government as a national launch site, and
offers Atlantic frontage on the opposite side of the continent — opening polar
and sun-synchronous access and a second ocean basin.

## Why three sites rather than one

No single launch site serves the whole orbital demand curve. Latitude and
geography decide which orbits are reachable efficiently, so the three locations
were selected to overlap as little as possible in what they cover, and as much as
possible in who they can serve.

Together they span 23.6°S to 8.5°N across two continents and three ocean
corridors, reaching low, mid and high inclinations — including sun-synchronous —
under a single operator, one compliance regime and one contract.

Dual-hemisphere operation also means a clear-weather site is available somewhere
in the network at any time of year, which reduces scrubs across the customer
base.

> Neither of these is a binding definitive agreement. Both remain subject to
> survey, licensing, regulatory approval and definitive documentation.
